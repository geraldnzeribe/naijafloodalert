2022: what riverside farmers could have known, and when

In 2022 Nigeria had its worst floods in a decade. Farmland along the Niger and Benue went under water just before harvest. We replayed that season using a model trained only on 2016–2020, as if it had been running at the time.

Why days of warning matter on a farm

Floodplain farmers grow rice, maize, sorghum, yam and cassava close to the river because the soil is rich. Most of those crops are mature or nearly mature in September and October, exactly when the Niger and Benue peak. With a week or two of warning a family can:

  • harvest early whatever is mature (rice and maize can be cut a little early and dried),
  • move stored grain, seed and fertiliser above the flood line,
  • move animals and equipment (pumps, sprayers) to high ground,
  • avoid new planting on the riverbank until the water falls.

With one or two days, there is little time to do more than leave.

Five places, replayed

Each chart shows the river's daily flow from July to November 2022 against its own Watch, Warning and Severe levels (from its long-term record), and the model's daily chance that the river would rise above Warning within 14 days.

What this shows, honestly

  • The big, slow floods were visible early. The September–October flood on the Niger (Niger State, Kebbi, Kogi, Delta, Bayelsa) was flagged 7 to 14+ days ahead. 14 days is the longest horizon the model tries, so some were flagged at least that early. These are the floods that stay for weeks and destroy harvests.
  • Sudden surges are still hard. The August surge on the Benue (Adamawa, Taraba, Benue, Nasarawa) was flagged only 0–2 days ahead. Watching water coming from upstream in Cameroon (our Lagdo Dam tracker) and adding rainfall forecasts is the next step to close that gap.
  • This is a replay on modelled river flows (Copernicus GloFAS), not on measured gauge readings or farm-loss records. Checking it against NIHSA gauges and farmer reports is the next step, and it is why we are looking for pilot partners.

Today, the same warning reaches farmers as plain advice

When a river near a farming community is forecast to rise, NaijaFloodAlert's Farmer view shows a harvest-before-the-flood advisory: how many days remain, which crops are usually ready for harvest in that zone at that time of year, and what to move. It works in English, Pidgin, Hausa, Yorùbá and Igbo, on 2G text pages and in the basic-phone (USSD) demo.
